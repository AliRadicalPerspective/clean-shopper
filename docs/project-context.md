## 1. Problem Statement
People who want to buy cleaner, non-toxic, and more sustainable home, pantry, and personal-care products struggle to do so because the research required is overwhelming and time-consuming. Product labels demand expertise to interpret, "clean" and "natural" marketing claims are frequently misleading, and the information needed to evaluate a product is scattered across many sources.

## 2. ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)
Someone actively trying to make more informed purchasing decisions for their home and family — replacing toxic cleaning products, finding personal-care items without harmful chemicals, or stocking a pantry with cleaner food. They are already motivated to research alternatives but currently do it manually across fragmented sources. They have accumulated preferences over time (brands they trust, ingredients they avoid) but no consistent place to store or apply them. They care about ingredient safety and certifications but are not necessarily experts at reading ingredient lists.

## 3. Pain Points
- Product labels are confusing and ingredient lists require expertise to interpret.
- "Clean" and "natural" claims on packaging are often misleading or unverified.
- Researching alternatives across multiple sources is fragmented and slow.
- Preferences built up over time — trusted brands, avoided ingredients — have nowhere to live.

## 4. Proposed Solution
Clean Shopper is a conversational AI agent, backed by Claude, that lets a user:
- Describe what they're looking for and receive researched product recommendations with reasoning, based on real-time web search and ingredient-safety databases (e.g., EWG's Skin Deep).
- Save preferences — ingredients to avoid, trusted brands, and certifications that matter (EWG Verified, USDA Organic, B Corp, etc.) — that are then applied automatically to every subsequent recommendation.
- Add recommended products to a shopping cart that persists across sessions.
- Ask for a side-by-side comparison of products and get a clear recommendation based on their saved preferences.

## 6. Design Constraints
Platform: Conversational (AI agent backed by Claude). Explicitly out of scope for V1: mobile app.
Geography: Not defined in source material.
Accessibility: Not defined in source material.
Technical: Real-time web search; ingredient-safety databases such as EWG's Skin Deep. Out of scope for V1: checkout or payment processing, direct retailer integrations, barcode scanning, and user accounts or authentication. (Note: the shopping cart must persist across sessions despite no user accounts — see Open Questions.)
Other: This is a course demo project for "Claude Code for Designers," built by students over four weeks using Claude Code, with the instructor demonstrating the workflow live each session.

## 7. Open Questions
1. How does the cart (and saved preferences) persist across sessions given that user accounts and authentication are explicitly out of scope for V1?
2. What defines the "clean standards" a product's ingredients are evaluated against — is this solely EWG data, a broader rubric, or a defined internal standard?
3. Which specific data sources beyond EWG's Skin Deep are in scope for ingredient safety and sustainability evaluation?
4. What is the intended scope of product categories at launch (cleaning, personal care, pantry/food — all at once, or phased)?

## 8. Gaps
1. **Success Metrics (Section 5)** — The brief defines no quantitative or qualitative measures of success. Without a target outcome (e.g., recommendations accepted, carts built, research time saved), it will be hard to judge whether design decisions are working or to prioritize features.
2. **Geography (Section 6)** — No launch region or regional restrictions are stated. This matters because ingredient regulations, available certifications, and product availability differ by region and could shape both data sources and recommendations.
3. **Accessibility (Section 6)** — No accessibility requirements are stated. A conversational interface still has significant accessibility considerations that should be established before design work begins.
4. **Persistence mechanism** — The brief requires the cart to persist across sessions while ruling out user accounts, but does not explain how identity/state is maintained. This directly affects the core UX and needs resolution early.
